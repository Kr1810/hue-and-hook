## Overview

Kitab Quarterly is an independent magazine about reading culture in India. I designed their "Shelf Life" issue, a collection of essays, interviews and photo stories about the city's oldest bookshops.

## Challenge

The issue mixed long essays, Q&As, photo essays and short listicles, and every format needed a clear identity without the magazine feeling disjointed. The budget allowed for only two-colour printing in the inner pages.

## Concept

*Paper, ink and dust jackets.* I used an ink-black and paper-yellow palette inspired by old book spines, a classic serif for reading comfort, and a sharp grotesque for headlines and pull quotes.

## Design process

- Built a 12-column grid with baseline alignment in InDesign
- Designed distinct templates for essays, interviews, photo stories and short reads
- Treated photographs in duotone to work within the two-colour budget
- Designed the cover as a "dust jacket" with a hidden illustration on the inside flap
- Proofed physical copies and adjusted type sizes and margins for real-world reading

## Final outcome

An 84-page print issue with a wraparound cover, six feature layouts, a duotone photo essay and a matching set of social and launch-event materials.
